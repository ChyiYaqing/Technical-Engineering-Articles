> Advice on getting started with technical and creative writing.

## Writing with no Experience

If you are someone who has little-to-no experience in writing, I would suggest two things-

**Read** (A lot, a helllll lot, and never stop)- Doesn't matter. Books, articles, quotes, poems; read them all. You can start your reading journey on platforms like Medium and Substack. Heck, even start reading those quotes on Pinterest if you have to. If you are into tech, read as many tech-related articles as you can. Get some decent beginner-level books and start reading (Way better than doomscrolling). Your vocabulary, pattern-recognition and speech skills will thank you. It'll also reduce mental overstimulation, so overall better performance everywhere. I have finished 11 books so far this year, and it was the best decision I made.

**Write** (no matter how juvenile your work feels) - The best way to do something is actually "doing" it. That's it. If you want to learn how to code, go and write some code. If you want to learn how to draw, get a sketchbook and draw something. It's the same with writing. Now you might argue - "I can't form words in my mind". That's a fair point. But the thing is, no one is asking you to write an 800-page novel in your first attempt. Say you want to improve your focus and lock in for 12 hours; it'll be quite unrealistic for someone to actually do it on the first attempt, especially if they lack focus. A reasonable and helpful approach would be to study for short intervals, train your brain to focus for a longer period of time, and gradually increase the duration. You can apply the same approach to your writing journey.

- Start by writing something small, 2-3 quotes, for example. Read more, and apply the learnings (like a new word you learnt).
- Gradually increase the length. Writing short articles. Platforms like Medium and Substack can be really helpful here. You can write all sorts of articles from 2-min reads to 20/30-min reads.
- Improvement should be there. So try to learn new writing patterns and words from other writers.
- Be consistent and do it for the love of the game.
- If your brain is completely blank, **start with journaling.** Get a diary, and just pen down how you felt and what you did during the entire day. Be fearless and honest here. Write like no one's going to read it. Not only will your mental blocks begin to disappear, but your emotional well-being will also improve through journaling. I ain't asking you to find a topic here. Just write about your day, your feelings, your dreams, your wins/losses and your ideas.

Yes, there are "formats" in all sorts of writing, but in the end, you get to choose and create your own FORMAT!!

I'm pretty sure these steps will help you a lot as a beginner, especially. Now, let's see what the difference is between technical writing and creative writing.

## Technical Writing

This side is more about knowledge and learning than your emotions. If you are planning to write your first technical article, don't let your mind linger over "selecting a proper pattern" or something like that. Just pick a topic you are comfortable with and knowledgeable about, make notes about it, arrange it accordingly, and write in a readable & crystal-clear way. No one cares about your impeccable vocabulary when it comes to writing technical articles.

- Pick a topic you are super confident about.
- Make your own rough notes.
- Here's an important point: "Many smart people don't want to actually help other people. They just want to show everyone how smart they are." Don't do this. Make sure your tech article is a) helpful to others & b) also good for self-revision.
- Write without caring about a "universal format".

## Creative Writing

This is more about your emotions, feelings and experiences rather than your "knowledge". Stuff like creative writing, drawing, etc. improve the way you "see" this world.

Example- Writers often use the fall season as a metaphor for letting go of the old, withered parts of ourselves.

There is no rule here. Just write, fellow mortal. Whatever you see, hear, feel or dream about, just pen it down. Don't worry about style or perfectionism. We are all in the same boat.

"But I don't have any topic to write about?"

There's a very interesting verse from an old, classic poem-

Biting my truant pen, beating myself for spite, "Fool," said my Muse to me, "look in thy heart, and write." - From **Loving in truth by Sir Philip Sidney**

So, when it comes to creative writing, you might need to seek help from the external world, but in the end, the writing has to come from the inside.

Just publish your first piece now!
